Parser generation

One grammar the whole hive can read

Waggle is an LL(k) parser generator for context-free grammars. From a single .waggle grammar it generates a recursive-descent parser and a full AST for Java, C++ and Rust — and the generated parsers carry no Waggle runtime dependency.

Waggle started as a fork of JavaCC 7.0.13 and keeps its proven model: LL(k) recursive descent, a token manager with lexical states, and syntactic and semantic lookahead. What changed is the surface — a simplified, EBNF-like syntax, and tree building written right in the grammar with #Node annotations, so there is no separate JJTree step.

Why “Waggle”? Honeybees talk in the waggle dance: a forager encodes the direction and distance of a food source in a figure-eight run, and the other bees in the hive parse the dance to find it. A grammar that others read — which is exactly what a parser generator is about.
